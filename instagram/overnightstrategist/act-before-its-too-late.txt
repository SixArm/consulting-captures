Why do businesses fail to change until it’s too late?

Most companies wait for clear proof before making bold moves.

By the time they get it?
The market’s already moved on.

It’s a classic trap: leaders want data to justify change, but the clearer the data, the smaller your window to act.

That’s the "Information-Action Paradox", developed by Siren, Anthony and Bhaat.

When the platform is burning, change becomes much harder. So, how do you get ahead of disruption before it hits?

Here’s a 4-step playbook:

(1) Generate Private Data

Public data won’t push your company to act. You need 'private data', the unique insights only you can gather.

• Interview top customers for deep feedback.
• Ask frontline staff what they’re hearing.
• Spot micro-trends in your own performance.

(2) Use Frameworks to Make Sense of Uncertainty

Uncertainty paralyses teams. Frameworks help them understand why acting now matters, even with imperfect data.

• Map your risk using tools like the disruption curve.
• Run workshops to stress-test assumptions.
• Debrief to build shared understanding.

(3) Build Collective Conviction

Your exec team might get it, but if the rest don’t, change stalls. You need everyone on board.

• Create safe spaces for debate.
• Run live polls to surface blind spots.
• Define key terms to avoid misalignment.

(4) Nudge New Behaviours

Change is hard. Nudges make it stick.

• Reward early adopters.
• Update KPIs to reinforce new habits.
• Celebrate quick wins.

The Takeaway:

Waiting for perfect data is a losing game.

The companies that win act early, build collective belief, and make change real - well before the real problem starts.
